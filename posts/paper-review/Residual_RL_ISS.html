1 Introduction

This report establishes a deterministic stability guarantee for a residual reinforcement-learning (RL) controller deployed on top of a nominal nonlinear model-predictive controller (NMPC) and a model-based disturbance observer (DOB). The combined NMPC–DOB layer is mature and admits a classical stability certificate; the RL layer, by contrast, is a neural-network policy and resists direct stability analysis.

Our strategy is to borrow the stability certificate of the baseline. We first show that the NMPC–DOB cascade is Input-to-State Stable (ISS) with respect to the uncompensated residual disturbance that escapes the observer’s passband. From the ISS inequality we extract a quantitative budget on the admissible residual, then map this budget back onto the RL action space as a hard, state-aware magnitude ceiling. Any RL torque within the ceiling is admissible by construction; any torque exceeding the ceiling is radially scaled back. The cage is built entirely from quantities of the certified baseline, so the learning agent inherits a provable stability margin without itself being verified.

The remainder of the document is organised as follows. Section 2 fixes notation and the cascade control law. Section 3 introduces the three layers. Section 4 states the standing assumptions that make the NMPC optimal cost a valid Lyapunov candidate. Section 5 derives the baseline ISS inequality. Section 6 is the core contribution: the ISS-based action-clipping law and its derivation via the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality. Section 7 describes how the three constants \(c_x\), \(\gamma_0\), and \(r\) are obtained from simulation and hardware experiments.

2 Setup and Notation

2.1 Tracking-error state and the cascade control law

We work in the discrete-time tracking-error coordinates of the 6-DOF manipulator. Let \(\bm{q}_k,\bm{v}_k \in \mathbb{R}^6\) denote the measured joint position and velocity at sample \(k\), and let \(\bm{q}^{\mathrm{ref}}_k,\bm{v}^{\mathrm{ref}}_k\) be the time-varying joint-space reference obtained by inverse kinematics of the Cartesian end-effector target. The tracking-error state is

\[ \bm{x}_k \;\triangleq\; \begin{bmatrix} \bm{q}_k - \bm{q}^{\mathrm{ref}}_k \\[2pt] \bm{v}_k - \bm{v}^{\mathrm{ref}}_k \end{bmatrix} \;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{12}. \tag{1}\]

The origin \(\bm{x}_k = \bm{0}\) denotes perfect tracking. Throughout this document, \(\|\,\cdot\,\|\) denotes the Euclidean norm.

The cascade control law executed by our implementation is

\[ \bm{u}_{\mathrm{cmd},k} \;=\; \underbrace{\bm{u}_{\mathrm{MPC},k}}_{\text{nominal NMPC}} \;-\; \underbrace{\bm{d}_{\mathrm{filter},k}}_{\text{DOB estimate}} \;-\; \underbrace{\bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL},k}}_{\text{residual RL}}, \tag{2}\]

where all three components live in the same physical joint-torque space \(\mathbb{R}^6\). The minus sign on the RL term reflects that the policy is trained to cancel the residual disturbance, mirroring the DOB.

2.2 Plant dynamics under the residual disturbance

Let \(\bm{f}(\bm{x},\bm{u})\) denote the nominal (URDF-modelled) closed-loop dynamics. The true plant, on real hardware or in simulation with friction and base excitation, evolves as

\[ \bm{x}_{k+1} \;=\; \bm{f}\bigl(\bm{x}_k,\bm{u}_{\mathrm{cmd},k}\bigr) \;+\; \bm{w}_k, \tag{3}\]

where \(\bm{w}_k\) collects all sources of model mismatch (un-modelled friction, base excitation, payload variation, parameter drift). The DOB estimates the in-band component of \(\bm{w}_k\) and subtracts it as \(\bm{d}_{\mathrm{filter},k}\); what the observer cannot capture remains as the residual disturbance

\[ \bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k} \;\triangleq\; \bm{w}_k \;-\; \bm{d}_{\mathrm{filter},k} \;\in\;\mathbb{R}^6. \tag{4}\]

The residual is the only exogenous input that appears in the forthcoming ISS analysis. We will frequently abuse notation and write \(\bm{x}_{k+1} = \bm{x}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{nom}} + \bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}\), where \(\bm{x}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{nom}}\) is the disturbance-free successor state under the nominal closed loop; this is the standard state-displacement embedding of an input disturbance and is sufficient for the Lyapunov analysis that follows.

3 The Cascade Architecture

3.1 Layer 1 — Nominal NMPC

The NMPC solves, at each sample \(k\), a finite-horizon optimal-control problem

\[ V_N^*(\bm{x}_k) \;=\; \min_{\bm{u}_{0:N-1}} \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} \ell\bigl(\bm{x}_{k+j},\bm{u}_{k+j}\bigr) \;+\; V_f\bigl(\bm{x}_{k+N}\bigr) \tag{5}\]

subject to the nominal dynamics \(\bm{x}_{j+1}=\bm{f}(\bm{x}_j,\bm{u}_j)\), input bounds, and (implicitly) a terminal set. The NMPC feedback law is the first element of the minimising sequence, \(\bm{u}_{\mathrm{MPC},k} = \kappa_N(\bm{x}_k) \triangleq \bm{u}^*_{0}(\bm{x}_k)\). In our implementation the stage cost is \[ \ell(\bm{x},\bm{u}) \;=\; Q_{\mathrm{pos}}\,\bigl\|\bm{p}(\bm{x})-\bm{p}^{\star}\bigr\|^2 + Q_{\mathrm{vel}}\,\bigl\|\bm{v}(\bm{x})\bigr\|^2 + R_{\tau}\,\|\bm{u}\|^2, \tag{6}\] with \(Q_{\mathrm{pos}}=5000\), \(Q_{\mathrm{vel}}=10\), \(R_{\tau}=0.1\), horizon \(N=8\), and sample time \(\Delta t = 0.02\)~s; \(\bm{p}(\bm{x})\) denotes the forward-kinematics end-effector position.

The optimal cost \(V_N^*\) defined in Eq. 5 will serve as our Lyapunov candidate.

3.2 Layer 2 — Model-based DOB

The DOB estimates the disturbance from the dynamics residual: \(\bm{d}_{\mathrm{raw},k} = \mathrm{RNEA}\bigl(\bm{q}_k,\bm{v}_k, \hat{\bm{a}}_k\bigr) - \bm{u}_{\mathrm{cmd},k-1}\), where \(\hat{\bm{a}}_k\) is an acceleration estimate (in simulation this is the ground-truth physical acceleration; on hardware it is the finite difference of \(\bm{v}_k\)). The raw estimate is passed through a first-order low-pass filter with smoothing factor \(\alpha \in (0,1)\):

\[ \bm{d}_{\mathrm{filter},k} \;=\; (1-\alpha)\,\bm{d}_{\mathrm{filter},k-1} \;+\; \alpha\,\bm{d}_{\mathrm{raw},k}. \tag{7}\]

The filter shapes which disturbances are cancelled and which fall through to the residual Eq. 4. Reducing \(\alpha\) tightens the passband, which enlarges the residual that the RL layer must handle.

3.3 Layer 3 — Residual RL

The RL policy \(\bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL},k} = \pi(\bm{o}_k)\) is a neural network that observes the same signals available to the DOB plus the DOB’s output, and emits an additive joint torque whose ideal target is the residual itself, i.e. \(\bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL},k} \to \bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}\). The policy is trained against a reward that penalises the residual \(\bm{d}_{\mathrm{eff},k} \triangleq \bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k} - \bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL},k}\) and tracking error. Because \(\pi\) is a learned, possibly stochastic, function, no closed-form stability statement is available for the closed loop that includes it. The role of Section 6 is to provide one.

4 Lyapunov Candidate: The Optimal NMPC Cost

We adopt the NMPC value function \(V_N^*\) from Eq. 5 as the composite Lyapunov candidate for the cascade. This choice is standard in the predictive-control literature and avoids the need to design a Lyapunov function by hand.

4.1 Standing assumptions

We collect the three structural assumptions used throughout the rest of the document. They are the standard hypotheses behind the quasi-infinite horizon NMPC stability theorem [@mayne2000; @rawlings2017], adapted to our cascade setting.

(A1)–(A3) are mild: (A1) follows from positive-definiteness and radial unboundedness of the cost; (A2) is design-driven and is automatically satisfied by the quadratic stage cost Eq. 6; (A3) holds locally because \(V_N^*\) is the value of a smooth, finite-horizon quadratic program on a compact set.

The constants \(c_x\) and \(\gamma_0\) that appear in (A2)–(A3) are exactly the two constants we will need to size the action-clipping law. How to obtain them in practice is the subject of Section 7.

4.2 Value-function descent (nominal stability)

Under (A1)–(A2) and the standard NMPC terminal ingredients (a control-invariant terminal set \(\mathcal{X}_f \subset \mathcal{X}\) with a local control-Lyapunov terminal cost \(V_f\)), the NMPC value function satisfies the well-known descent property along the nominal closed loop.

Lemma 1 Lemma 1 (Value-function descent). Under Assumption 1 and the standard NMPC terminal assumptions, the optimal cost \(V_N^*\) satisfies \[ V_N^*\bigl(\bm{x}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{nom}}\bigr) - V_N^*(\bm{x}_k) \;\le\; -\ell\bigl(\bm{x}_k,\kappa_N(\bm{x}_k)\bigr) \;\le\; -c_x\,\|\bm{x}_k\|^2 \tag{8}\] for every \(\bm{x}_k \in \mathcal{X}\), where \(\bm{x}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{nom}} = \bm{f}\bigl(\bm{x}_k,\kappa_N(\bm{x}_k)\bigr)\) is the disturbance-free successor.

NoteProof sketch (standard)

The argument is the canonical “tail-shift” of NMPC stability theory [@mayne2000, Thm. 1; @rawlings2017, §2.4]. From \(\bm{x}_k\) the optimal plan costs \(V_N^*(\bm{x}_k)\) over \(N\) steps. After applying its first input the system reaches \(\bm{x}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{nom}}\); re-using the tail of the previous plan and appending the terminal controller yields a feasible (not necessarily optimal) plan from \(\bm{x}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{nom}}\) whose cost is at most \(V_N^*(\bm{x}_k) - \ell(\bm{x}_k,\kappa_N(\bm{x}_k))\). The re-optimised value \(V_N^*(\bm{x}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{nom}})\) cannot exceed this feasible cost, which proves the first inequality. Applying (A2) proves the second.

Lemma 1 isolates the intrinsic convergence strength of the controller: every disturbance-free step strips at least \(c_x\|\bm{x}_k\|^2\) units of cost. The constant \(c_x\) is the convergence coefficient of the nominal loop and is the first ingredient of the action-clipping law.

5 Input-to-State Stability of the Baseline

We now activate the residual disturbance and the DOB, keeping \(\bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL},k} = \bm{0}\) (i.e., the baseline is the NMPC–DOB cascade alone). The goal is to certify ISS of the baseline with respect to \(\bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}\).

5.1 Cost propagation under the residual disturbance

The true successor state under the residual disturbance is \[ \bm{x}_{k+1} \;=\; \bm{x}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{nom}} \;+\; \bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}. \tag{9}\]

The one-step change of the Lyapunov candidate decomposes by adding and subtracting \(V_N^*(\bm{x}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{nom}})\):

\[ \begin{aligned} V_N^*(\bm{x}_{k+1}) - V_N^*(\bm{x}_k) &= \underbrace{\bigl[V_N^*(\bm{x}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{nom}} + \bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}) - V_N^*(\bm{x}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{nom}})\bigr]}_{\text{perturbation term}} \\[4pt] &\quad+ \underbrace{\bigl[V_N^*(\bm{x}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{nom}}) - V_N^*(\bm{x}_k)\bigr]}_{\text{nominal descent term}}. \end{aligned} \tag{10}\]

This decomposition is an identity: nothing has been bounded yet. Its purpose is to isolate the only place the disturbance enters—the first bracket—from the part already controlled by Lemma 1.

5.2 Lipschitz bound on the perturbation term

The first bracket of Eq. 10 is bounded directly by Assumption (A3): \[ V_N^*\bigl(\bm{x}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{nom}} + \bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}\bigr) - V_N^*\bigl(\bm{x}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{nom}}\bigr) \;\le\; \gamma_0\,\bigl\|\bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}\bigr\|. \tag{11}\]

The constant \(\gamma_0\) is the disturbance gain of the cascade. It plays the role of an “exchange rate” from units of state displacement (metres of error) to units of cost. A large \(\gamma_0\) means the cost landscape is steep near the operating trajectory and the system is sensitive to small disturbances; a small \(\gamma_0\) means the cost surface is flat and the system is tolerant.

5.3 The baseline ISS inequality

Combining Lemma 1 and Eq. 11 yields the central result of this section.

Theorem 1 Theorem 1 (Baseline ISS). Under Assumption 1 and the standard NMPC terminal assumptions, the NMPC–DOB baseline cascade (i.e., Eq. 2 with \(\bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL}}=\bm{0}\)) satisfies, on the operating domain \(\mathcal{X}\), \[ V_N^*(\bm{x}_{k+1}) - V_N^*(\bm{x}_k) \;\le\; -c_x\,\|\bm{x}_k\|^2 \;+\; \gamma_0\,\|\bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}\|. \tag{12}\] Equivalently, the baseline is Input-to-State Stable with respect to the residual disturbance \(\bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}\), with ISS-Lyapunov function \(V_N^*\), dissipation rate \(\alpha_3(s)=c_x s^2\), and disturbance gain \(\sigma(s)=\gamma_0 s\).

NoteProof

The decomposition Eq. 10 is an identity. Bound its first bracket using Eq. 11 and its second using Eq. 8; sum the two bounds. The resulting inequality Eq. 12 matches the ISS-Lyapunov template of Jiang & Wang [@jiang2001, Thm. 1], so ISS follows immediately. \(\square\)

Eq. 12 is the quantitative statement of stability. The first term on the right is the energy drain due to the controller; it scales like \(\|\bm{x}_k\|^2\) and dominates far from the origin. The second term is the energy refill due to the disturbance; it scales linearly with \(\|\bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}\|\). Equilibrium between drain and refill sets the steady-state radius of the tracking error.

6 ISS-Based Action Clipping

We now use the budget hidden inside Eq. 12 to constrain the RL action. The argument proceeds in four steps: (i) extract the admissible residual budget from the ISS inequality; (ii) write the ISS inequality for the closed loop with the RL layer; (iii) impose a preservation criterion that the RL action must not enlarge the residual it is meant to shrink; (iv) reduce that criterion, via the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, to a hard magnitude ceiling on \(\bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL},k}\).

6.1 The admissible residual budget

Fix a desired practical convergence radius \(r > 0\): we want the tracking error to settle inside \(\|\bm{x}_k\| \le r\). From Eq. 12, the cost stops contracting precisely when the drain no longer dominates the refill, i.e. when \(c_x\,\|\bm{x}_k\|^2 \le \gamma_0\,\|\bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}\|\). To force this balance to occur at or inside the target radius, the refill at \(\|\bm{x}_k\|=r\) must not exceed the drain there: \[ \gamma_0\,\|\bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}\| \;\le\; c_x\,r^2 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \|\bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}\| \;\le\; \bar{d}_{\mathrm{res}} \;\triangleq\; \frac{c_x\,r^2}{\gamma_0}. \tag{13}\]

Definition 1 Definition 1 (Admissible residual budget). The scalar \(\bar{d}_{\mathrm{res}} \triangleq c_x r^2 / \gamma_0\) is the admissible residual budget: the largest residual norm that the baseline NMPC–DOB cascade can absorb while keeping the tracking-error state within radius \(r\).

The three quantities entering Eq. 13 have crisp roles: \(c_x\) rewards strong convergence (larger \(c_x\) enlarges the budget), \(r\) rewards looser performance targets (the budget grows like \(r^2\)), and \(\gamma_0\) punishes sensitivity to disturbance (a steeper cost surface shrinks the budget).

6.2 ISS inequality with the RL layer active

With \(\bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL},k} \ne \bm{0}\), the effective disturbance seen by the baseline is shifted: \[ \bm{d}_{\mathrm{eff},k} \;\triangleq\; \bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k} - \bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL},k}, \tag{14}\] and the ISS inequality Eq. 12 becomes \[ V_N^*(\bm{x}_{k+1}) - V_N^*(\bm{x}_k) \;\le\; -c_x\,\|\bm{x}_k\|^2 \;+\; \gamma_0\,\|\bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k} - \bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL},k}\|. \tag{15}\]

6.3 The stability-preservation criterion

We declare that the RL layer is admissible at step \(k\) whenever it does not enlarge the residual it is intended to cancel: \[ \boxed{\quad \|\bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k} - \bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL},k}\| \;\le\; \|\bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}\|. \quad} \tag{16}\]

If Eq. 16 holds, then Eq. 15 is no worse than Eq. 12, so the baseline ISS certificate carries over verbatim and the closed loop remains ISS with the same constants.

6.4 From the preservation criterion to a magnitude bound

Eq. 16 is geometric and depends on the unmeasurable internal quantity \(\bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}\). We now reduce it to a measurable condition on \(\|\bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL},k}\|\) alone, in three algebraic steps.

Step 1 — Square both sides. Squaring Eq. 16 and expanding the inner product on the left: \[ \|\bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}\|^2 - 2\,\bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}^{\!\top}\bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL},k} + \|\bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL},k}\|^2 \;\le\; \|\bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}\|^2, \] which simplifies to \[ \|\bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL},k}\|^2 \;\le\; 2\,\bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}^{\!\top}\bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL},k}. \tag{17}\]

Step 2 — Cauchy–Schwarz, with the known bound. The inner product on the right of Eq. 17 depends on the unmeasurable \(\bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}\). Writing \(\bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}^{\!\top}\bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL},k} = \|\bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}\|\,\|\bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL},k}\|\cos\theta\) with \(\theta \in [0,\pi]\) the angle between the two vectors, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives \(\cos\theta \le 1\), and the residual budget Eq. 13 gives \(\|\bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}\| \le \bar{d}_{\mathrm{res}}\). Together, \[ \bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}^{\!\top}\bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL},k} \;\le\; \|\bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}\|\,\|\bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL},k}\| \;\le\; \bar{d}_{\mathrm{res}}\,\|\bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL},k}\|. \tag{18}\]

Step 3 — Solve for \(\|\bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL},k}\|\). Substituting Eq. 18 into Eq. 17, \[ \|\bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL},k}\|^2 \;\le\; 2\,\bar{d}_{\mathrm{res}}\,\|\bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL},k}\|. \] Dividing both sides by \(\|\bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL},k}\| > 0\) (the case \(\bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL},k}=\bm{0}\) is trivial) yields the ceiling.

Theorem 2 Theorem 2 (ISS action-magnitude ceiling). Suppose Assumption 1 holds and the baseline satisfies Theorem 1. For any step \(k\) at which the preservation criterion Eq. 16 is demanded, the RL action magnitude must satisfy \[ \|\bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL},k}\| \;\le\; 2\,\bar{d}_{\mathrm{res}} \;=\; \frac{2\,c_x\,r^2}{\gamma_0}. \tag{19}\]

6.5 The clipping law

The ceiling Eq. 19 is enforced as a stateless, radial saturation layer interposed between the actor network and the joint command. If the raw output already satisfies the ceiling, it passes through unchanged; otherwise it is scaled radially back onto the boundary of the admissible ball:

Definition 2 Definition 2 (ISS clipping law). The clipped RL torque applied to the cascade is \[ \boxed{\quad \bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL},k}^{\mathrm{clip}} \;=\; \bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL},k}\cdot \min\!\Bigl[\,1,\; \frac{2\,c_x\,r^2}{\gamma_0\,\|\bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL},k}\|} \Bigr]. \quad} \tag{20}\]

Two properties of Eq. 20 deserve emphasis:

  1. Direction-preserving. The scaling is radial, so a well-aimed but over-strong correction is softened, never redirected. This is important because the policy’s reward function rewards alignment of \(\bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL}}\) with \(\bm{d}_{\mathrm{res}}\); per-component clamping would destroy that alignment.

  2. Stateless. The clip depends only on the constants \(c_x,\gamma_0,r\) (fixed once and for all) and on the current \(\bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL},k}\). It does not depend on the state \(\bm{x}_k\) or on the residual \(\bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}\). This makes the clip cheap on hardware and trivial to implement.

6.6 Geometric interpretation and honest caveat

Eq. 19 is a necessary condition for Eq. 16 to be satisfiable; it is not a blanket sufficient condition. Reading Eq. 17 exactly, the preservation criterion holds if and only if \(\|\bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL},k}\| \le 2\,\|\bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}\|\,\cos\theta\). The largest value the right-hand side can take, over all admissible residuals and all angles, is \(2\,\bar{d}_{\mathrm{res}}\), attained only when the action is perfectly aligned with the residual at the worst-case magnitude. Thus \(2\,\bar{d}_{\mathrm{res}}\) is the outer envelope: any action exceeding it cannot satisfy Eq. 16 under any alignment, and is therefore provably unsafe; the clip removes exactly these actions.

Inside the envelope, alignment is the policy’s job, not the clip’s. What the clip guarantees, even in the adversarial worst case where the clipped action is anti-aligned with the residual, is a hard upper bound on the effective residual via the triangle inequality: \[ \|\bm{d}_{\mathrm{eff},k}\| \;\le\; \|\bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}\| + \|\bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL},k}^{\mathrm{clip}}\| \;\le\; \bar{d}_{\mathrm{res}} + 2\,\bar{d}_{\mathrm{res}} \;=\; 3\,\bar{d}_{\mathrm{res}}. \] Plugging \(3\,\bar{d}_{\mathrm{res}}\) back into the ISS inequality Eq. 12 in place of \(\|\bm{d}_{\mathrm{res}}\|\) shows that the closed loop remains ISS with an enlarged—but still finite—ultimate radius \(\sqrt{3}\,r\). The cage never opens.

TipWhat the clip guarantees, in one sentence

If the RL policy aligns with the residual (its trained behaviour), the error settles inside the target radius \(r\); if the policy fails arbitrarily, the error stays inside \(\sqrt{3}\,r\).

7 Practical Estimation of the ISS Constants

The clipping law Eq. 20 contains three constants: \(c_x\), \(\gamma_0\), and \(r\). This section explains how each is obtained.

7.1 Convergence coefficient \(c_x\)

By Assumption (A2), \(c_x\) is the largest scalar satisfying \(\ell(\bm{x},\kappa_N(\bm{x})) \ge c_x \|\bm{x}\|^2\). Equivalently, by Lemma 1, \(c_x\) is the largest scalar consistent with the descent property \[ \frac{V_N^*(\bm{x}_k) - V_N^*(\bm{x}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{nom}})} {\|\bm{x}_k\|^2} \;\ge\; c_x \quad\text{for all nominal } \bm{x}_k\in\mathcal{X}. \tag{21}\]

A nominal trajectory is one in which the disturbance is small or absent; on real hardware we approximate “nominal” by quiet operating conditions (no intentional disturbance, no external excitation, no payload changes). The empirical estimator is then

Definition 3 Definition 3 (Empirical convergence coefficient). Let \(\{(\bm{x}_k, V_N^*(\bm{x}_k))\}_{k=1}^{K}\) be a sequence of state–cost pairs logged from quiet closed-loop operation. The empirical convergence coefficient is the lower 5th percentile, \[ \hat{c}_x \;=\; \mathrm{percentile}_{5}\!\Biggl\{\; \frac{V_N^*(\bm{x}_k) - V_N^*(\bm{x}_{k+1})} {\|\bm{x}_k\|^2} \;:\; \|\bm{x}_k\| > \epsilon,\;\; V_N^*(\bm{x}_k) > V_N^*(\bm{x}_{k+1}) \;\Biggr\}, \tag{22}\] where \(\epsilon\) is a small floor (e.g. \(10^{-3}\,\)m) preventing the ratio from exploding near the origin.

The use of the 5th percentile rather than the strict minimum trades off two competing concerns: the strict minimum is contaminated by isolated numerical outliers (e.g. MPC solves that hit the iteration cap), while the median over-estimates the descent and would violate the lower-bound interpretation for a non-trivial fraction of samples. The 5th percentile gives a robust lower envelope that is safe in the ISS sense (i.e. a valid lower bound for \(\ge 95\%\) of samples).

This procedure can be applied identically in simulation and on real hardware. We recommend taking \(\hat{c}_x = \min(\hat{c}_x^{\mathrm{sim}},\hat{c}_x^{\mathrm{real}})\) to absorb the discrepancy caused by unmodelled friction on the physical plant.

7.2 Disturbance gain \(\gamma_0\)

By Assumption (A3) and the construction of Eq. 11, \(\gamma_0\) is the smallest scalar that upper-bounds the cost change induced by a state displacement of the magnitude of \(\bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}\). Re-arranging the ISS inequality Eq. 12, \[ \gamma_0 \;\ge\; \frac{-\bigl[V_N^*(\bm{x}_{k+1}) - V_N^*(\bm{x}_k)\bigr] \;+\; c_x\,\|\bm{x}_k\|^2} {\|\bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}\|} \quad\text{whenever } \|\bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}\| > 0. \tag{23}\]

The numerator is computable from logged \(V_N^*\) and \(\|\bm{x}_k\|\); the denominator requires the clean ground-truth residual. This is the critical practical asymmetry:

  • In simulation, \(\bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}\) is computable exactly from \(\bm{d}_{\mathrm{true},k}^{\mathrm{sim}} - \bm{d}_{\mathrm{filter},k}\), where the “true” disturbance is obtained by inverse dynamics with the ground-truth joint acceleration provided by the physics engine.
  • On real hardware, the joint acceleration is reconstructed by finite differences, which injects numerical noise into the residual estimate. The empirical fit Eq. 23 would under-estimate \(\gamma_0\) (because the noisy denominator is inflated), which is the unsafe direction.

The remedy is to estimate \(\gamma_0\) in simulation only, then transfer the value to real hardware. The transfer is exact because \(\gamma_0\) is a property of the cost function \(V_N^*\), and \(V_N^*\) is the same mathematical function in sim and real (same NMPC weights, same URDF, same horizon, same sample time).

Definition 4 Definition 4 (Empirical disturbance gain). Let \(\{(\bm{x}_k, V_N^*(\bm{x}_k), \bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}^{\mathrm{sim}})\}_{k=1}^{K}\) be logged from a baseline simulation run with the full disturbance curriculum active. The empirical disturbance gain is the 95th percentile, \[ \hat{\gamma}_0 \;=\; \mathrm{percentile}_{95}\!\Biggl\{\; \frac{-\Delta V_k + \hat{c}_x\,\|\bm{x}_k\|^2} {\|\bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}^{\mathrm{sim}}\|} \;:\; \|\bm{d}_{\mathrm{res},k}^{\mathrm{sim}}\| > \delta, \;\;\text{(ratio)} > 0 \;\Biggr\}, \] where \(\Delta V_k = V_N^*(\bm{x}_{k+1}) - V_N^*(\bm{x}_k)\) and \(\delta\) is a small floor preventing division by near-zero residuals.

The 95th percentile—rather than the strict maximum—gives a robust upper envelope in the same way the 5th percentile gave a robust lower envelope for \(\hat{c}_x\).

7.3 Target radius \(r\)

The radius \(r\) is the only design quantity in the clipping law; it is not measured but selected. It encodes the practical tracking accuracy the user is willing to certify. Three observations guide the choice:

  1. The clip ceiling scales as \(r^2\). Halving \(r\) shrinks the ceiling by a factor of four; doubling \(r\) enlarges it sixteen-fold. Aggressive (small) \(r\) yields a tighter guarantee but allows the RL layer almost no contribution; permissive (large) \(r\) enlarges the guarantee region but lets the policy be more active.

  2. \(r\) should be consistent with what the baseline can already achieve. If the baseline NMPC–DOB tracking error is observed to settle around \(\|\bm{x}_k\| \approx r_{\mathrm{base}}\) in steady state, then choosing \(r \ll r_{\mathrm{base}}\) certifies an unattainable guarantee and forces the clip to be uselessly tight. A pragmatic choice is \(r \approx r_{\mathrm{base}}\), or slightly smaller if the RL layer is expected to close the gap.

  3. \(r\) does not appear in the ISS certificate of Theorem 1 itself. It only appears in the budget Eq. 13 and the ceiling Eq. 19. ISS holds independently of \(r\); the choice of \(r\) only specifies which radius the budget targets.

7.4 Hybrid sim–real workflow

Putting the three estimators together:

Constant Where measured Why
\(c_x\) both, take \(\min\) property of NMPC + plant; real may be smaller due to friction
\(\gamma_0\) simulation only property of \(V_N^*\) alone; transfers exactly to real
\(r\) design choice not measured

The final clip ceiling used at run-time is \[ \|\bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL}}\| \;\le\; \frac{2\,\hat{c}_x\,r^2}{\hat{\gamma}_0}, \qquad \hat{c}_x = \min(\hat{c}_x^{\mathrm{sim}}, \hat{c}_x^{\mathrm{real}}), \quad \hat{\gamma}_0 = \hat{\gamma}_0^{\mathrm{sim}}. \tag{24}\]

Because the strict minimum of \(c_x\) and the strict maximum form of \(\gamma_0\) are both taken in the conservative direction, all estimation error is absorbed by a small reduction in the clip ceiling. Stability is therefore preserved under estimation error; only the RL layer’s freedom is reduced. This is the desired robustness property of the proposed certificate.

8 Summary

Under the standing assumptions Assumption 1, the NMPC–DOB cascade is ISS with respect to the residual disturbance (Theorem 1), with quadratic dissipation rate \(c_x\) and linear disturbance gain \(\gamma_0\). For any target radius \(r > 0\), the admissible residual budget \(\bar{d}_{\mathrm{res}} = c_x r^2/\gamma_0\) specifies the largest residual the baseline can absorb while keeping the tracking error inside \(r\). The residual RL layer is admissible at step \(k\) if it does not enlarge the residual it is meant to cancel; this preservation criterion reduces, via the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, to the magnitude ceiling \(\|\bm{u}_{\mathrm{RL},k}\| \le 2 c_x r^2/\gamma_0\) (Theorem 2) and the radial clipping law Eq. 20. Provably-unsafe actions—those whose magnitude exceeds \(2\bar{d}_{\mathrm{res}}\) and cannot satisfy the preservation criterion under any alignment—are removed by construction. Inside the envelope, even adversarial misalignment of the clipped action leaves the closed loop ISS with enlarged ultimate radius \(\sqrt{3}\,r\); the cage never opens. The three constants \(c_x, \gamma_0, r\) are obtained via Definition 3, Definition 4, and the design rule of Section 7.3; the resulting hybrid sim–real workflow Eq. 24 is robust to estimation error in the conservative direction.
